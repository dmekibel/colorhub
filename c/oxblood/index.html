Oxblood or Burgundy?

Darker than burgundy, a nearly black red.

Compare: Burgundy

Art

Chinese potters called it Lang kiln red; Europeans called it sang de boeuf, oxblood. This copper-red glaze was developed around 1705 to 1712, under the Kangxi emperor, to revive a lost Ming red. Copper only turns red in a kiln starved of oxygen, the same trick that turns celadon green, and the results were so unpredictable that Western potters spent decades trying to copy it.

Culture

On 1 April 1960 Dr. Martens launched its 1460 boot in cherry red, a deep oxblood. Skinheads took it up in the late 1960s, then punks in the 1970s.

Kin

  • Celadon: China's other great glaze; both need a kiln starved of oxygen
  • Brick: Kiln chemistry again: oxygen decides fired clay's color
  • Burgundy: Two near-black reds, one named for wine and one for blood
