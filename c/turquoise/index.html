Turquoise or Aqua?

Greener and a little deeper than aqua.

Compare: Aqua

Language

Turquoise means Turkish. The stone came from mines in Persia and reached Europe through Turkish lands, so the French called it the Turkish stone, though none was mined in Turkey.

History

Ancient Egyptians sent expeditions to the Sinai for turquoise. At the mines of Serabit el-Khadim they built a temple to Hathor, who was called the lady of turquoise. Half a world away, Aztec artisans covered masks, and at least one human skull, in turquoise mosaic.

Science

Turquoise is a copper mineral, like malachite, and copper gives it the sky color. A little iron in the mix pushes it greener.

Art

The Maya made a turquoise-blue paint that has survived centuries of jungle damp. Maya blue is indigo locked inside a rare clay, palygorskite. For a long time people assumed a blue-green that refused to fade must be a metal pigment; how it really works was pinned down only around 2000.

Kin

  • Malachite: Another stone colored by copper, prized in ancient Egypt
  • Azure: Persia's other famous blue stone, lapis lazuli, gave us azure
  • Teal: Named after a duck's eye patch of nearly the same blue-green
