Byzantium or Plum?

Darker than plum.

Compare: Plum

Named after the Byzantine Empire and its imperial purple.

History

Byzantine emperors kept the finest purple for themselves, and sumptuary laws barred everyone else. Children born to a reigning emperor were porphyrogennetos, born in the purple: delivered in a palace room lined with purple porphyry stone. See royal-purple.

Art

In the mosaics of San Vitale in Ravenna, finished in 547, the emperor Justinian stands in a purple cloak on a gold ground, his courtiers around him in white.

Kin

  • Purple: The emperor's color, a Byzantine monopoly
  • Gold: Byzantine mosaics set imperial purple on gold
