Sienna or Rust?

Duller and browner than rust.

Compare: Rust

History

Sienna is earth from Tuscany, dug on Monte Amiata in lands once ruled by the city of Siena. Raw sienna is a yellow-brown iron earth; roast it, the iron turns to hematite, and you get red-brown burnt sienna. Renaissance painters made it standard, and the Italian deposits were largely worked out by the 1940s. See earth-pigments.

Language

Sienna is recorded as a color name in English from 1760, and burnt sienna from 1853.

Kin

  • Umber: Its darker partner in every painter's box of earths
  • Ochre: Iron earths: heat turns yellow ochre red and raw sienna burnt
  • Burnt orange: Another color 'burnt' darker and redder
