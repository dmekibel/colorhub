His life in color

Three places, three palettes · 4 min read

Netherlands · 1881–1885

The peat-dark years

He began among farm workers, painting in Brownish Olive, Sepia and near-black. The Potato Eaters is lit by one lamp; almost everything else is Smoky Black.

Paris · 1886–1887

The palette opens

In Paris he met Impressionist and Neo-Impressionist work. Greys turned silvery, Pale Russian Blue and Grey-Green crept in, and the browns thinned out.

“By 1890 he used dark brown a third as often as painters of his time.”

Arles & Saint-Rémy · 1888–1890

Yellow against blue

In the south he set Cadmium Lemon against Cyanine Blue, a pair he wrote about to his brother. Some of his chrome yellows have since darkened, so the sunflowers you see are browner than he painted them.
